Most families arrive at a memory clinic with the same two questions. What is going on, and what do we do about it. Cognitive care often answers those slowly and out of order. You get a test here, a scan there, and a plan that never quite arrives.

Mindspan runs care in three stages, in a fixed order: Discover, Decide, Care. Each stage has one job. Each ends with something you can hold. This article walks through all three.

## Before the first visit

The work starts before your first visit.

- **Intake.** A member of our care team calls you. They take your history, ask what you and your family have noticed, and collect records from your other doctors. Bring the name of your primary care clinician and a list of medications, including supplements.
- **A memory check at home.** You complete a short cognitive check on a laptop or tablet, at your own pace. It is a screening, not a diagnosis. It gives your care team a starting point.
- **Preparation.** Before the visit, our team reviews what has come in: your history, the check, any prior labs or imaging. If something is missing, we chase it so the visit is not spent on paperwork.

## Stage one: Discover

The Discover visit is your first appointment with your Mindspan care team. It is a long visit by design.

Your care team goes through your history with you and with someone who knows you well. We ask for that person to be in the room, or on the call, because the people around you often notice changes before you do. A clinician on the team examines you, reviews the records, and orders what is needed to get a clear picture. That usually means blood tests and brain imaging. Where it helps, it includes newer blood biomarkers.

At a Discover visit you also complete cognitive testing with a trained member of the team. This is more detailed than the check you did at home. It takes time, and it is the part families most often say they wish had happened years earlier.

You leave the Discover visit knowing three things: what has been ordered, why, and when you will hear from us.

## Stage two: Decide

Once the results are back, you come in for the Decide visit.

The visit opens with your neurologist interpreting the cognitive testing alongside the labs and imaging. Then comes the conversation most families have been waiting for: what we found, what it means, and what the options are. Sometimes the answer is reassuring. Sometimes it is a diagnosis. Either way, you hear it in plain language, with your family in the room, and with time to ask questions.

If the findings point to early Alzheimer's disease, this is where your neurologist talks through whether the newer treatments for it fit your situation. These medicines have specific eligibility criteria and known risks, and the decision is made together, not handed down.

You leave the Decide visit with a diagnosis or a clear explanation of why there is not one yet, and with an agreed direction.

## Stage three: Care

Care begins with a care plan visit. Before it, your neurologist and care team draft a written plan from everything learned so far. At the visit, you go through it together.

A care plan covers more than medicine. It addresses safety at home, driving, medications that may be clouding thinking, mood, sleep, hearing and vision, and the practical questions families usually have to figure out alone. It names who on the care team you call, and for what. Caregivers are part of this visit on purpose. The plan is theirs as much as the patient's.

After the care plan visit, care continues on a schedule that fits your situation. For most people that is a visit every three to six months, in clinic or from home, with the care team available between visits. Things change. The plan changes with them.

## Why three stages

The order matters. A plan built before the diagnosis is a guess. A diagnosis delivered without a plan is a door closing. Discover gathers the evidence, Decide reads it, Care acts on it. At every point you know which stage you are in and what the next one is.

## What to bring to a first visit

- A list of medications, including supplements
- Contact details for your primary care clinician and any specialists
- A timeline of what you have noticed, with examples
- Someone who knows you well

If you are not yet a patient and want to know whether a first visit makes sense, start with the [memory check at home](https://assessment.mindspan.co/). If you already have a visit booked, our team will reach out before it with what to expect.
